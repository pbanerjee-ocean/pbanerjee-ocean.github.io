About Me

My research is interdisciplinary, encompassing two broad themes: (i) investigating long-term changes in atmospheric aerosols (mainly dust) and (ii) exploring various problems pertaining to ocean biogeochemical cycles. I use a combination of remote sensing, in situ data, a regional climate model, as well as an Earth system model to address science questions.

I received my Bachelor and Masters degree from University of Calcutta in Geography. I hold a Ph.D. in Marine Sciences from CSIR-National Institute of Oceanography, India (2015). During Ph.D., I studied seasonal cycle and interannual variability of dust aerosol over the Middle East and South Asia. I also explored impacts of dust deposition on phytoplankton growth over the northwestern Indian Ocean using remote sensing data and RegCM model.

I was at the Indian Institute of Science as a Project Scientist during 2016–2018 and as a DST Inspire Faculty Fellow during 2019–2024. My work involved setting up aerosol measurement systems at multiple sites across the northern Indian Ocean to study aerosol trace metals impacts on upper ocean biogeochemistry. I have also conducted simulations with CESM2 to study the ocean iron cycle and impacts of dust deposition on ocean productivity and carbon sink.

Currently, at Dartmouth College my research involves assessing the efficacy of dust fertilization as a marine-based carbon dioxide removal strategy.
